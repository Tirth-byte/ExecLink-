# Product Taste

## Mission
Prevent ExecLink from looking like a generic AI-generated hackathon dashboard. Make deliberate product decisions with restraint, hierarchy, clarity, and professional density.

## Apply When
Any visible UI, information hierarchy, interaction, copy, layout, or component is being created or reviewed.

## Rules
- Optimize for serious infrastructure/project-controls software, not marketing-site spectacle.
- Establish one obvious primary purpose per screen.
- Prefer strong hierarchy and whitespace over decoration.
- Use fewer, more meaningful metrics instead of KPI-card soup.
- Tables, filters, evidence, timelines, and operational states are first-class UI.
- Use accent color sparingly to communicate priority/action/state.
- Avoid giant headings, excessive gradients, glass everywhere, neon glows, random illustrations, excessive rounded cards, and “AI sparkle” decoration.
- Make dense information scannable rather than artificially sparse.
- Every visible element must earn its space.

## Taste Check
Ask:
1. Does this look plausible in a serious EPC/infrastructure organization?
2. Is the most important information obvious in five seconds?
3. Is anything decorative competing with operational information?
4. Could removing an element improve the screen?
5. Does this feel intentionally designed rather than component-library assembled?

If the answer exposes weakness, fix it before adding more UI.
